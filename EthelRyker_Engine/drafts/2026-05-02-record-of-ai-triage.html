Record of AI Triage

02 May 2026

When a headline says AI is better than doctors at triage, someone in an emergency room feels a quiet shift. A question forms about the hands reaching for them. The people trying to give care feel it too. Their expertise, the years spent learning to read a body, suddenly made less.

The story being told is not about what is ready now. It is about a different kind of extraction. Attention. Investment. A future that isn't here yet, marketed as if it were. This narrative is built on the silence around what real care costs, the nuanced choices, the human judgement that weighs every variable.

The damage lands on the people needing help, already vulnerable. And on the clinicians. It makes trust harder. It makes the work heavier.

— Ethel Ryker
