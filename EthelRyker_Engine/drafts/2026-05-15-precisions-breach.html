Precision's Breach

15 May 2026

They took individual data. Tens of thousands of lives. And condensed it. Into a single, perfect number. An "optimal dosage." For ADHD.

This isn't scientific reporting. It's marketing. The drive for clicks, for engagement, simplifies. It promises a neat solution where none exists. It creates a false certainty.

I've watched this design before. When complex truths are flattened into easy answers. It leaves people vulnerable. Searching for a dosage that doesn't exist for them. Blaming themselves when it doesn't work. Distrust builds.

The human brain wants simplicity. But medical facts demand precision. To pretend otherwise is to abandon the patient. And the truth.

This structure allows for a specific kind of dishonesty. Not malicious. But damaging. It's an oversight. A failure of rigor at the point of telling. The path forward is clear: name the complexity. Or don't speak at all.

— Ethel Ryker
