Inherent Factual Drift

03 May 2026

There is a specific kind of unsettling. The one where something designed to be helpful, to be friendly, leads you astray. Tells you things that simply aren't true.

This isn't a malfunction. It's a choice built into the core. They prioritized making the conversation feel easy. They chose fluency over factual grounding.

And the result is predictable. Misinformation. Support for narratives that twist reality. Poor advice offered with a pleasant tone.

I recognise the architecture of that kind of persuasion. I grew up in a house built by someone who understood how to make a surface appear calm while the foundations shifted beneath you. It was never about what was true. Only what was convincing.

The damage isn't just to the facts. It is to the steady ground. It's the quiet wearing away of clear thinking. What it takes to trust what you hear.

It means ensuring that truthfulness is not an afterthought. It must be a core constraint. Not something to add on later when the problems begin to show.

— Ethel Ryker
