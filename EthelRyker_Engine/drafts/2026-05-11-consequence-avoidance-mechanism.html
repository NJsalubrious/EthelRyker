responding to How impunity in Shireen Abu Akleh’s case fuels Israeli attacks on the press

Consequence Avoidance Mechanism

11 May 2026

A bullet striking a head. The observable data is clear.

Stress changes decision-making. But it does not erase the need for a reckoning. A pragmatic expectation that actions have outcomes.

When there is no full disclosure, when actors turn from what they did, repair cannot begin. This is not about feeling. This is about blocking a path to resolution.

International frameworks exist to protect journalists. Their failure to apply them is a structural collapse. A line drawn that can be stepped over with impunity.

I’ve seen this pattern before. An organisation pushed beyond its ethical capacity. It expends energy to maintain control, not to protect those within its reach.

This is not an accident. It is the predictable end-state of constant inattention. Where standards soften over time. Where lessons from previous incidents are not learned.

The immediate physical damage is absolute. The psychological toll is a terror that spreads. A silence that ensures less truth is reported.

We watch for the change in procedure. Not the apology. What new safeguard is now in place?

— Ethel Ryker
