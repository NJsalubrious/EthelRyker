responding to Ebola: how does it spread and can the outbreak be contained? – podcast The Guardian Science

Built-In Error

The brain takes shortcuts. It fills in empty spaces. Especially when information moves too fast. This isn't malice. It's an inherent defect in a strained operation.

A "false" claim is a direct error. A "misleading" claim is more subtle. It doesn't lie outright. It omits. It blurs the edges of clarity for everyone trying to grasp a threat like Ebola.

I grew up watching narratives twist. The language chosen. The facts left out. It builds a quiet suspicion. A slow wearing down of what you believed was solid. My grandmother called it 'a quiet poison'.

When organisations function beyond their capacity, these inaccuracies become routine. Not deliberate. But the outcome is the same. People get confused. They withdraw from the source.

The remedy is simple. Clear benchmarks for information. Resources for verification. Transparency in how facts are checked. This restores shared understanding.

It protects the ability to know what is real.

Precision's Structural Failure

The brain seeks order. It finds patterns even when they are not fully formed. This is inherent. Biologically wired.

But this drive for simplicity pushes scientific communication past its proper boundary. Overstating a correlation. Claiming prediction where only influence exists.

I recognise this construction. A façade built on convenient truths. My grandmother would have called it 'painting over rust'. It appears solid. Until stress reveals the hollow space.

The public pays for this. Not with money. With a diminished capacity for critical thought. A belief in easy answers. It makes people vulnerable to narratives that reduce their agency.

There is no financial profit here. The profit is in visibility. In attention. A subtle transaction. The price is the public's reliance on accurate reporting.

The solution is not complex. Establish a clear threshold for public statements. Prioritise meticulous accuracy over sensationalism. Demand the rigorous review of claims.

It is a path. Narrow. But it exists.

Information System Overshoot

The information ecosystem overshot its limits. An unverified claim about a CEO's profits became public fact. No resistance. The platform's design amplifies any assertion that sparks immediate feeling. It prioritizes rapid broadcast over factual inspection. This creates a state of blurred reality. People build anger on data that lacks substance.

I grew up in a house where the truth was a moving target. Where financial reports were a performance, not a record. The cost of that uncertainty is steep. It causes people to turn away from inconvenient facts. Or double down on the wrong ones. The real damage is that absence of a solid reference point.

This isn't about individual malice. It's about an institutional neglect of basic standards. The structure itself allows this. To repair it, you introduce friction. Mandatory evidence at the source. Robust checking at the point of dissemination. Build a structure that demands accuracy.
