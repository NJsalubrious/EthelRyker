Someone Will Believe This

02 May 2026

Someone living with cancer reads "cancer-free" and feels the familiar jolt. They know the word does not mean what it says, or what it takes to stay alive.

The headline flattens a complex medical reality. It offers a narrative of cure when the truth is often ongoing treatment, careful monitoring, and a different kind of survival. It is easier to sell a clear ending than the long work of living with illness.

That cost lands on people still fighting their own battles. It makes their lived experience harder to explain, harder to validate. It leaves a silence where accurate understanding should be. People need to know what a body really does to survive, and what it costs.

— Ethel Ryker
