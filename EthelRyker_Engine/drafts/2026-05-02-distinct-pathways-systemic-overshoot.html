Distinct Pathways, Systemic Overshoot

When people are trying to heal, they need a map that leads somewhere real. We now understand that combining alcohol with cocaine changes the brain’s specific pathways for relapse. This is vital. It means a generalized approach, designed for cocaine alone, might not reach the true source of the struggle.

But then the public message gets tangled. An unsupported comparison gets added, an extra claim that isn't verified. This isn't a small thing. It creates an epistemic inaccuracy, a slippage in the truth. I grew up watching my grandmother insist on precision in the lab. "A single stray digit changes everything," she'd say. The data had to be clean, without a single unproven assertion.

The consequence of this imprecision is real. It means individuals are offered a treatment plan built on something not quite true for them. Their healing is prolonged. It is a failure in structural clarity, not malice. But the price of that failure is paid in human perseverance, in the repeated attempts to find a path through a landscape that was misdescribed.

What we need is communication that matches the evidence. Not oversimplified. Not embellished. Just the facts, stated with their boundaries. The path to repair begins with knowing exactly what you are repairing.

— Ethel Ryker
