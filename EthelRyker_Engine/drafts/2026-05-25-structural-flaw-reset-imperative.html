Structural Flaw, Reset Imperative

25 May 2026

The National Anti-Corruption Commissioner has departed. The agency is publicly described as "troubled."

This is not a personal failing. It is a structural defect. When a critical public body cannot function, the damage extends beyond its walls. It impacts the public's belief in oversight.

The language used is important. Calling an agency "troubled" obscures the precise decisions that starved it of resources or burdened it with an unmanageable scope. This deflects scrutiny from those who designed its initial conditions.

My grandmother would have seen this pattern. A new initiative, intended to protect, but quietly under-resourced. Its eventual failing then blamed on the people trying to operate it.

True repair requires an honest assessment of actual capacity versus its stated purpose. It demands specific identification of what was withheld. Not just a new face at the top.

The operational integrity of this body is at stake. The path forward is through precise data. Not broad statements. Not excuses.

— Ethel Ryker
