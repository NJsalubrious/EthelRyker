Silence as Sanction

19 May 2026

Silence can be a barrier. A wall. But often, it is a weapon. A deliberate withdrawal of connection, designed to inflict a specific kind of wound.

The absence of sound becomes a constant demand for interpretation. For a child, this creates a vacuum. A space where self-worth is consumed by the effort to decipher an unstated fault. They learn to search for meaning in empty rooms. To carry the burden of an uncommunicated charge.

I grew up in an environment where quiet was a tactic. Where the loudest message was the one never spoken. It forces an internalisation. A profound sense of isolation that outlasts the actual quiet.

This is not just a failure of care. It is a predictable outcome when the capacity for emotional fluency runs dry. When learned patterns of relational deprivation repeat across generations. The social compact demands something different. It requires a specific kind of responsibility. To name the void. To choose engagement.

The damage is clear. The toll of being human inside that particular omission is immense. The choice to repair it is also clear. It begins with speech.

— Ethel Ryker
