Epistemic System Failure

16 May 2026

False claims about executive orders. A presidential endorsement of psychedelics. A non-existent directive. This is not a minor error. It distorts public reality.

People prioritize what sounds engaging. Not what is verifiably true. This creates a feedback loop. Algorithms amplify it. They reward clicks over factual accuracy. The outcome is a corrupted public record. It makes informed judgment impossible.

I grew up in a house where meticulous observation was bedrock. Where a single inaccurate measurement could ruin an entire experiment. This current drift is the opposite. It normalises inaccuracy. It turns away from the empirical.

When information flows too fast, verification becomes a burden. It is easier to publish unverified claims than to stop and check. This is not always malice. It is often simple fatigue. A lack of funding. A choice to prioritise speed over correctness.

Repair means pointing to the specific lie. Not attacking the publisher. It demands transparent correction. It requires a decision to value truth.

— Ethel Ryker
