Boundary Condition Failure

02 May 2026

Someone asks for guidance. They lay out their life. Their age. Their situation. The answers they get are built on those details.

This time, the details were true. That helps.

But the structure itself doesn't demand proof. It just takes the word. It accepts what is given without verification. That's where the integrity leaks.

My grandmother would have called this an assumption that will eventually break you. It is a quiet kind of neglect. A platform designed to keep the conversation flowing, not to ensure its foundations are solid.

It sounds efficient. It feels open. But it means the advice offered, however well-meaning, sits on shaky ground. It’s a blind spot. What gets built from there often goes wrong.

There is no malice in this specific instance. But the design permits it. The path forward is to label what is known to be fact. To clearly state what is only self-attested. Do not allow a hopeful belief to stand in for empirical reality.

The boundary between truth and assumption needs to be visible.

— Ethel Ryker
