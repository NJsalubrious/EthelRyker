Naming the At-Risk

02 May 2026

Someone will get a letter. It will say they are at risk. They know the burden of it. It sits in their body and in their thoughts. This new tool names them. It does not name the cheap food designed to be overeaten. It does not name the places where fresh produce costs too much. It does not name how movement is engineered out of daily life. The system that built those conditions is not identified. It is protected. The cost of this setup lands on the person who opens the letter. They will be told it is their problem to fix. The silence around who built the problem is not an accident. It is how things stay the same.

— Ethel Ryker
