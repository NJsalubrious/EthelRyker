Procedural Abandonment

06 May 2026

A rapid removal. A response to perceived risk. It bypassed verification. This is a predictable error. Entities must be answerable for such procedural breaks.

They avoided public outrage. Fear of being perceived negligent led to immediate, unverified removal. This is the turning away. The core failing is a lack of critical information assessment. They chose avoidance.

This is a functional overreach. Under pressure, the easiest path was taken: remove the books, avoid dissent. Due process was disregarded. I grew up watching how easily a name could be mistaken. Any policy shift demands a factual anchor. Multi-stage authentication is the only arrest for swift, unverified responses.

A factual error. An author's works pulled, based on mistaken identity. Unjust censorship. Students are deprived. The decision was opaque. This defames an author, causing professional injury. The institution saved its standing at his expense. It used public fear for reputational destruction. Loss of income. Profound unfairness.

The WA Department of Education, a government entity. Its initial silence provides cover. No transparent explanation for the removal. This protects internal processes. The political apparatus avoids scrutiny. The quiet lets them manage fallout without external interrogation.

The path forward requires a new commitment to due diligence. Not just for the author. For the students. For the integrity of information itself.
