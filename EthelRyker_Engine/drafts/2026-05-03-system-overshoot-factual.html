System Overshoot: Factual

03 May 2026

There is a specific kind of disorientation when you learn something you believed was real never existed. People invest their attention. Their concern. Their hope, even. Only to find it was directed at thin air.

This isn't about malice. It’s about a process overwhelmed. Too much noise. Too little checking. Facts get lost in the rush. Or they never form at all.

My grandmother would have said this was an unmanaged process. A machine running beyond its capacity. It leaves a void where certainty should be.

But this kind of public misdirection holds a quiet advantage for some. When a non-existent inquiry seems to be in motion, it can take the urgency out of demanding a real one. It looks like action. Without requiring any. This is how public expectation can be gently steered away from what actually needs doing.

The price of this informational drift is paid in public belief. It makes people question everything. It makes them tired. It makes them stop looking.

The only repair for a non-fact is a clear, loud declaration of its absence.

Nothing less will do.

— Ethel Ryker
