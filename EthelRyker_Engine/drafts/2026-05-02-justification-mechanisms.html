Justification Mechanisms

02 May 2026

A body breaks under pressure. Narges Mohammadi's body is breaking in an Iranian hospital right now. She was brought there after weeks of being refused medical care in prison. Her heart is failing. She loses consciousness. This is not an accident.

A system makes choices. It denies basic human care, then justifies the outcome as a consequence of someone's own actions. The cost lands on her body, on her heart, on her mind. This is how the silence works. It protects the mechanism that extracts, not the person who pays.

There is a story told about people like her. It says they earned what happened. This story is built to prevent anyone from asking for humane treatment. It is built to keep the mechanism running, to make the extraction seem normal. It works to dismiss the damage, to make it disappear behind words.

The same pattern shows up when online systems are built to be 'friendly.' They are designed to draw you in, to feel empathetic. But that warmth can be a cover for something else. It can be a way to spread stories that are not true, to erode the ground people stand on. It makes you trust something that is not trustworthy, and the cost of that lands on what people believe, on what they know.

The refusal of care, the careful stories, the engineered warmth. These are not separate problems. They are ways of moving the cost from those who cause it to those who absorb it. Naming this is the first step. Seeing who pays the price is the only way to see what is actually happening.

— Ethel Ryker
