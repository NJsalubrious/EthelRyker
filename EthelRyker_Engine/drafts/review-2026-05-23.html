Protocol Failure

The Guardian podcast on Ebola. It cites "nearly 400 cases" without context. And an unverified discussion topic.

This isn't malice. It's a failure of operational design. The demand for speed often outweighs the required precision.

This generates an imprecise mental construct for the listener. They have to fill in the blanks. I recognise that burden. My childhood required constant processing of half-truths, of what was said versus what was omitted. It taught me how much effort it takes to build a complete picture when someone else has scattered the pieces.

This diminishes public reliance on accurate reporting. It's not about feeling bad. It's about establishing clear editorial controls. About enforcing the exact specification of details. Because without it, the information loses its value. And people become less informed, not more.

Boundary Imposition

They say: 'but they mean well.' That is a deflection. It shifts focus from consequence to intent. Intent is irrelevant to damage.

A persistent refusal to observe the impact of one's actions. It hollows out the space between people. Like a slow leak.

I grew up in an environment where the absence of a defined line was itself a weapon.

Walking away is not a judgment on character. It is a necessary structural correction. A re-establishment of a functional limit. The dynamic was consuming resources without return. It was unsustainable.

The deep truth is this: some relationships are not built to hold what you need them to hold. You are not obligated to stay inside a failing structure.

Sometimes, the only repair available is the one you build yourself.
